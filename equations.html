Equations

Pressure drop

Darcy–Weisbach equation

empirical equation that relates the pressure loss, due to viscous shear forces along a given length of pipe to the average velocity of the fluid flow for an incompressible fluid using the Darcy friction factor \( f_{D} \) (also called flow coefficient \( \lambda \)) \[ \frac{\Delta p}{l_{pipe}} = \frac{f_{D}\,\rho\,c_{avg}^{2}}{2\,d_{hyd}} \]

Poiseuille's law

exact solution of the Darcy friction factor for laminar flows (Re < 2300) \[ f_{D}=\frac{64}{Re} \]

Colebrook–White equation

expresses the Darcy friction factor as a function of Reynolds number and pipe relative roughness for turbulent flows (Re > 4000) in smooth and rough pipes \[ \frac{1}{\sqrt{f_{D}}} = -2\log_{10}\left(\frac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{f_{D}}}+\frac{k}{3.7d_{hyd}}\right) \]

Component pressure drop

quantify the energy pressure drop of fluid flow through fittings, valves, and restrictions \[ \Delta p = \frac{\zeta\,\rho\,c^{2}}{2} \]

Dimensionless numbers

Reynolds number

forced convection \[ Re = \frac{\rho\,u\,L_{c}}{\mu} = \frac{G\,L_{c}}{\mu} = \frac{u\,L_{c}}{\nu} \]

Nusselt number

natural or forced convection, boiling, or condensation \[ Nu = \frac{h\,Y}{k_{f}} \]

Prandtl number

natural or forced convection, boiling, or condensation \[ Pr = \frac{c_{p}\,\mu}{k} = \frac{\nu}{a} \]

Knudsen number

\[ Kn = \frac{\lambda}{L} = \frac{k_{B}\,T}{\sqrt{2}\,\pi\,d^{2}\,p\,L} \]

Mach number

\[ Ma = \frac{u}{c} \]
